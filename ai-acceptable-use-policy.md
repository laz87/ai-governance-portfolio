## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes guidelines for the responsible and secure use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools and systems within [Company Name]. It ensures that AI adoption aligns with legal requirements, organizational values, and risk management principles.

This policy complies with:
- Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019
- Kenya AI Bill 2026 (proposed)
- EU AI Act (if applicable)
- NIST AI Risk Management Framework

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:
- All employees, contractors, and interns of [Company Name]
- All AI tools, platforms, and systems used for work purposes
- All data processed by AI systems on behalf of [Company Name]
- Third-party AI services accessed using company resources

**Covered AI tools include, but are not limited to:**
- Large Language Models (LLMs) — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, etc.
- AI-powered code assistants — GitHub Copilot, Cursor, etc.
- AI image/video generation tools — DALL-E, Midjourney, etc.
- AI analytics and automation platforms
- Custom AI models developed or fine-tuned by [Company Name]

## 4. Data Handling Rules

### 4.1 Data Classification

| Classification | AI Use | Examples |
|---------------|--------|----------|
| **Public** | ✅ Allowed | Marketing materials, public reports |
| **Internal** | ⚠️ With caution | Internal processes, non-sensitive docs |
| **Confidential** | ❌ Prohibited | Financial data, strategy documents |
| **Restricted** | ❌ Prohibited | PII, health data, credentials, trade secrets |

### 4.2 Prohibited Data Inputs

**NEVER input the following into any AI tool:**
- Customer personal data (names, IDs, phone numbers, addresses)
- Employee personal data (salaries, performance reviews, IDs)
- Financial information (account numbers, transaction details)
- Source code containing secrets, API keys, or credentials
- Legal documents under privilege
- Health or medical records
- Government-issued identification numbers
- Any data classified as Confidential or Restricted

### 4.3 Data Minimization

- Use only the minimum data necessary for the AI task
- Anonymize or pseudonymize data before input when possible
- Do not upload entire databases or large datasets to AI tools
- Delete AI-generated outputs containing sensitive data after use

### 4.4 Data Retention

- AI-generated content stored on company systems follows standard data retention policies
- Do not store AI outputs containing sensitive data in personal accounts
- Clear chat history with AI tools after each session where applicable

## 5. Employee Responsibilities

### 5.1 Required Actions

Every employee using AI tools must:
1. **Complete AI training** — Attend mandatory AI awareness training before using any AI tool
2. **Use approved tools only** — Do not use unapproved AI services for work
3. **Verify AI outputs** — Never rely solely on AI-generated content; verify accuracy
4. **Disclose AI use** — Clearly label AI-generated content when shared externally
5. **Report incidents** — Immediately report any AI-related security or compliance concerns
6. **Follow data rules** — Never input prohibited data into AI tools

### 5.2 Prohibited Actions

Employees must NOT:
- Use AI tools to bypass security controls or access restrictions
- Input confidential or restricted data into AI tools
- Share AI-generated content containing company secrets externally
- Use AI to generate phishing emails, social engineering content, or malicious code
- Use AI to impersonate colleagues, clients, or company representatives
- Rely on AI for decisions that require human judgment (e.g., hiring, firing, medical)
- Use AI to generate content that violates copyright or intellectual property rights

## 6. AI-Generated Content

### 6.1 Quality Assurance

- All AI-generated content must be reviewed by a human before use
- Technical content must be verified by a subject matter expert
- Customer-facing content must be approved by the communications team
- Code generated by AI must be reviewed and tested before deployment

### 6.2 Attribution & Disclosure

- Clearly label content as "AI-assisted" or "AI-generated" when appropriate
- Do not claim AI-generated content as original human work
- Disclose AI use in external communications when required by law or ethics

### 6.3 Intellectual Property

- Verify that AI-generated content does not infringe third-party IP
- Company owns all AI-generated content created using approved tools for work purposes
- Do not use AI to reproduce copyrighted material verbatim

---

## 7. Security Requirements

### 7.1 Access Control

- AI tool accounts must use company-managed identities (SSO where available)
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA) must be enabled on all AI tool accounts
- Access to AI tools follows the principle of least privilege

### 7.2 Monitoring

- The IT security team may monitor AI tool usage for compliance
- Automated alerts will be configured for suspicious AI usage patterns
- Regular audits of AI tool usage will be conducted

### 7.3 Incident Response

If you suspect an AI-related security incident:
1. **Stop** using the AI tool immediately
2. **Report** to the IT security team at [security@company.com]
3. **Document** what data was input and what output was generated
4. **Preserve** evidence — do not delete chat history or outputs
5. **Cooperate** with the incident response team

---

## 8. AI in Decision-Making

### 8.1 Human-in-the-Loop

AI may **support** decisions but must not **replace** human judgment in:
- Hiring, promotion, or termination decisions
- Customer credit or loan approvals
- Medical or health-related decisions
- Legal or compliance decisions
- Security incident response decisions

### 8.2 Bias Awareness

- Be aware that AI systems may produce biased outputs
- If you notice biased or discriminatory AI output, report it immediately
- Do not use AI outputs that could disadvantage protected groups

### 8.3 Accountability

- The human who uses AI output is accountable for the final decision
- AI is a tool, not a decision-maker
- Document when and how AI was used in significant decisions

## 9. Training & Awareness

### 9.1 Mandatory Training

All employees must complete:
- **AI Awareness Training** — Annual, covering risks, policies, and best practices
- **Role-Specific Training** — For employees who use AI regularly in their work
- **Incident Response Training** — For IT and security teams

### 9.2 Ongoing Education

- Monthly AI security tips via email newsletter
- Quarterly updates on AI policy changes and new threats
- Access to AI governance resources on the company intranet

## 10. Third-Party AI Services

### 10.1 Vendor Assessment

Before adopting any third-party AI service:
- Complete the **Vendor AI Due Diligence Checklist** (see separate document)
- Obtain a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) if personal data is involved
- Verify the vendor's security certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001)
- Confirm data ownership and deletion rights

### 10.2 Contract Requirements

All AI vendor contracts must include:
- Data privacy and protection clauses
- Model transparency requirements
- Security incident notification obligations
- Data deletion upon contract termination
- Right to audit security practices
